Why People are Your Best Security Control

When we think about cybersecurity, we often focus on complex malware, firewalls, and zero-day exploits. Yet, some of the most challenging vulnerabilities originate from within our organisations. The basic framework of ISO/IEC 27000:2018 (Section 3) (ISO/IEC, 2018) indicates that managing human behaviour is not just an HR task but a core pillar of information security governance.​

To build a human-centric defence, we must examine how standard definitions apply to our workforce.

Asset: Employees are the most vital organisational asset, as they represent anything that holds value for the organisation. Although technical systems have immense value, people drive innovation, execute daily processes, and maintain customer trust.

Threat: A threat is a potential cause of an undesirable incident. Internal actors, whether malicious, disgruntled, or fatigued and careless, can pose significant internal security risks.

Vulnerability: A lack of cybersecurity awareness creates human vulnerability (a weakness of an asset or control that can be exploited), turning a well-meaning staff member into an accidental entry point for phishing or social engineering.

Control: Implementing a robust control (a measure that modifies risk) means deploying ongoing education, clear policies, and a supportive reporting environment, rather than relying solely on restrictive technical barriers.

Information Security Incident: Empowering staff to speak up early reduces the likelihood of single or multiple unwanted events escalating into a full-scale information security incident.

Overcoming internal cyber-attacks requires shifting the corporate narrative from blame to empowerment. When security teams collaborate closely with employees, they no longer see the workforce as an organisational weakness but as an active, vigilant line of defence. Although technology can block malicious code, only people can build a truly resilient culture.
